# The story on one page: negative result, diagnosis, fix, limits

(Tier 0.2 of the Part D plan. Every number is from the sealed reports named in brackets; the claims are worded the way the plan asks: "cheaper than RK45 at equal marginal risk or at a certified tolerance", never "faster than adaptive solvers"; "integrator error inside the CR3BP", never "accurate trajectories".)

**The question.** In the Earth-Moon circular restricted three-body problem, can a model look at an initial state and horizon and pick ONE step size for a fixed-step integrator, before integrating, such that the run is cheap and its final position is within 100 km of a high-accuracy reference with a stated, certified risk? The hypothesis behind the project was that the finite-time Lyapunov exponent (FTLE), the distance to the primaries and the Jacobi constant govern how hard an orbit is.

**A. The negative result (Part A, preregistered, sealed 2026-10-23).** With the costs counted honestly (the FTLE alone costs about 70 times a DOP853 solve, so it cannot be a pre-simulation feature; the model costs RHS-equivalents too) the ML policy was not cheaper than an adaptive solver, and dropping FTLE from the features changed nothing measurable.

**B. The diagnosis (Part B, sealed 2026-10-25).** The cost gap against a per-orbit-tuned RK45 (+1.41 dex, a factor of 26) was taken apart into paradigm +0.21, selection +0.03, prediction +0.38, safety scale +0.30 and overhead +0.50 dex. The paradigm term, the best a single fixed physical step could do even with perfect knowledge, grows with the range of timescales along the orbit (Spearman 0.91): a fixed step is wrong in kind near a body. The ML policy was cheaper than an adaptive solver on 0 of 14,161 orbits.

**C. The fix (Part C, sealed 2026-10-27).** Integrate in Sundman time, where the step shrinks automatically near the bodies, and predict ONE number per orbit, the fictitious step, from the initial state and thirteen analytic two-body periapsis features, with a 25-tree model that costs 15 RHS-equivalents and an end-to-end certified safety scale. The paradigm term vanishes (-0.005 dex) and the total gap against RK45-spec falls to +0.21 dex. The policy is cheaper than an equal-risk RK45 on 71.4 % [70.6, 72.2] of orbits, at 4.4 % realized risk; it is not cheaper than the per-orbit oracle tolerance (8.7 %) and it loses on long chaotic horizons. [`part_c/RESULTS_PART_C.md`]

**D. What the result is worth (Part D, this round).** Five questions, five pre-specified tests, written before the data were used (`part_d/PREREGISTRATION_D.md`, sealed 2026-10-28):


- **D1, is the ML needed?** Mostly the periapsis information, partly the model: the best four-coefficient physics rule on the same free quantities is 0.072 dex (about 18 %) more expensive than the 25-tree policy and carries about three quarters of the saving over a single global step; a linear model on the 33 engineered features ties the trees. Supported.
- **D2, does it matter in wall-clock?** Not in total. Against a compiled DP5(4) at equal risk the policy is faster on 78 % of the orbits and 1.6 times faster at horizons of 1-3 TU, but its throughput over the 1-20 TU population is 0.885 [0.862, 0.908] of the adaptive solver's (0.64 against DOP853): the long chaotic horizons dominate the total. Not supported. The first measurement (0.58) was an implementation artefact that the RHS count had hidden.
- **D3, does it replicate?** Yes: cheaper than equal-risk RK45 on 71.6 % [70.8, 72.4] of 12,253 new orbits at 2.7 % risk. Supported.
- **D4, is it safe where it counts?** Conditionally, yes, on free quantities: 17 strata certified separately, all at or below 3.1 % violations, still cheaper than equal-risk RK45 on 66 % of the orbits; the marginal certificate of Part C had hidden 8.7 % at long horizons and 13.1 % at close lunar passes. Against RK45 certified in the same strata the policy wins on 56 %, against DOP853 on 24 %. Supported.
- **D5, does it generalize?** No. On the JPL periodic-orbit families the frozen policy violates in 70 % of the runs (vertical and axial families about 100 %) because its step is 0.3-0.5 dex too long there; recalibrating the scale brings the risk to 4 % at a cost above RK45's. Not supported.
- **The hypothesis the project started from** (FTLE, proximity, Jacobi constant): after the time transformation the horizon and the analytic periapsis features carry the information (D6); FTLE explains where the policy fails after the fact (AUC 0.92-0.94 on a surface of section, D7) but costs an integration and cannot be a feature.


**Limits that survive Part D.** the oracle-tolerance adaptive solver and DOP853 are not beaten; the wall-clock gain exists only at short horizons; the policy is not safe off its training distribution (structured orbits) and its risk concentrates in the high-lambda*T tail (25 %, D12) and at close lunar passes (about 5 % even with the conditional certificate); one system, one tolerance, one horizon range; the CR3BP is not the real Earth-Moon system (2,000-50,000 km against 100 km). Wording: "cheaper than RK45 at equal marginal risk or at a certified tolerance", never "faster than adaptive solvers".
